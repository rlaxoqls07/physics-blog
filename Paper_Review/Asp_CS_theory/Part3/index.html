Part 3: Nonabelian Gauge Transformations and Topology

While the abelian Maxwell-Chern-Simons theory elegantly generates topological mass without a Higgs field, the true geometric profundity of Chern-Simons theory reveals itself only when we upgrade the gauge symmetry to a nonabelian Lie group. In the nonabelian regime, the gauge field self-interacts, and the topology of the gauge group itself strictly dictates the permissible values of the coupling constant.

In this article, we rigorously analyze the nonabelian Chern-Simons action, track its highly non-trivial transformation under large gauge transformations, and derive the exact topological quantization condition that must hold for the quantum path integral to remain globally well-defined.

1. Lie Algebra Representations and Structural Preliminaries

1.1 The Gauge Algebra and Its Generators

Let \(\mathcal{G}\) denote the gauge Lie algebra with a chosen finite-dimensional representation. The gauge field \(A_\mu\) is a \(\mathcal{G}\) -valued one-form, expanded on the basis of generators \(\{T^a\}\) :

\[ A_\mu = A_\mu^a\, T^a, \]

where \(A_\mu^a\) are real-valued component fields. The generators close under the Lie bracket according to the structure constants \(f^{abc}\) :

\[ [T^a, T^b] = f^{abc}\, T^c. \]

Definition 3.1.1 (Anti-Hermitian Convention). We adopt the strict anti-Hermitian convention for the generators, normalizing the trace as:

\[ \mathrm{tr}\!\left(T^a T^b\right) = -\frac{1}{2}\,\delta^{ab}. \]

The negative sign signals that \(T^a = -\,(T^a)^\dagger\) , ensuring that the group element \(g = \exp(\theta^a T^a)\) is strictly unitary without the clutter of explicit factors of \(i\) .

1.2 Trace Identities Required Downstream

Two consequences of this normalization will be used repeatedly. First, contracting the bracket with a third generator:

\[ \mathrm{tr}\!\left(T^a[T^b,T^c]\right) = f^{bcd}\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left(T^a T^d\right) = -\frac{1}{2}\,f^{bca} = -\frac{1}{2}\,f^{abc}, \]

where the last equality exploits the total antisymmetry of \(f^{abc}\) (a consequence of the invariance of the Killing form for compact groups). Hence, the fully antisymmetrized triple-trace is:

\[ \mathrm{tr}\!\left(T^{[a} T^{b} T^{c]}\right) = -\frac{1}{4}\,f^{abc}. \]

Second, we heavily rely on the cyclicity of the trace, which, when contracted with the totally antisymmetric \(\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\) , permits free cyclic reordering of the gauge fields.

2. The Nonabelian Chern–Simons Action

2.1 Definition

Definition 3.2.1 (Nonabelian Chern-Simons Action). The nonabelian Chern-Simons Lagrangian density is given by:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{CS} = \kappa\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left( A_\mu \partial_\nu A_\rho + \frac{2}{3}\, A_\mu A_\nu A_\rho \right), \]

which translates into differential-form notation ( \(A = A_\mu\,dx^\mu\) ) as:

\[ \mathcal{S}_{CS} = \kappa \int_M \mathrm{tr}\!\left( A\wedge dA + \frac{2}{3}\, A\wedge A \wedge A \right). \]

2.2 Verification of the Cubic Coefficient

Why the precise factor of \(\tfrac{2}{3}\) ? We can rigorously prove that this specific coefficient is strictly required to yield the correct field equations ( \(F = 0\) ) under arbitrary variations.

Theorem 3.2.1 (Equation of Motion and the Cubic Coefficient). Retain the cubic coefficient as an unknown variable \(c\) and vary \(A \to A + \delta A\) on a manifold \(M\) with boundary:

\[ \mathrm{CS}_c(A) \equiv \mathrm{tr}\!\left( A\wedge dA + c\, A\wedge A\wedge A \right). \]

Using integration by parts \(\mathrm{tr}(A\wedge d\,\delta A) = \mathrm{tr}(\delta A\wedge dA) + (\text{bdy})\) and trace cyclicity, the variation is:

\[ \begin{aligned} \delta \mathcal{S}_{CS} &= \kappa \int_M \mathrm{tr}\!\Big( \delta A\wedge dA + A\wedge d\,\delta A + 3c\, \delta A \wedge A \wedge A \Big) \\ &= \kappa \int_M \mathrm{tr}\!\Big( 2\,\delta A\wedge dA + 3c\, \delta A \wedge A \wedge A \Big) + (\text{bdy}). \end{aligned} \]

To assemble this bulk term into \(\mathrm{tr}(\delta A\wedge F)\) , where the nonabelian field strength is \(F = dA + A\wedge A\) , we strictly require \(3c/2 = 1\) , which forces \(c = \tfrac{2}{3}\) .

With \(c = \tfrac{2}{3}\) , the variation cleanly reduces to:

\[ \delta \mathcal{S}_{CS} = 2\kappa \int_M \mathrm{tr}\!\left( \delta A\wedge F \right) + (\text{bdy}). \]

The bulk equation of motion is therefore exactly the zero-curvature condition \(F = 0\) . \(\blacksquare\)

3. Behavior Under Nonabelian Gauge Transformations

3.1 The Transformation Law

Under a group element \(g: M \to G\) , the nonabelian gauge field transforms as:

\[ A_\mu \to A_\mu^g \equiv g^{-1} A_\mu\, g + g^{-1} \partial_\mu g. \]

In differential form language, \(A \to A^g = g^{-1} A g + g^{-1} dg\) . This inhomogeneous term is geometrically profound.

Definition 3.3.1 (Maurer-Cartan Form). We introduce the (left-invariant) Maurer-Cartan form , which is an intrinsically \(\mathcal{G}\) -valued 1-form:

\[ \theta \equiv g^{-1} dg. \]

Theorem 3.3.2 (Maurer-Cartan Identity). The Maurer-Cartan form identically satisfies \(d\theta = -\,\theta \wedge \theta\) .

Proof. By differentiating the inverse identity \(g^{-1} g = \mathbb{1}\) , we obtain:

\[ \begin{aligned} 0 &= d\!\left(g^{-1} g\right) = \left(dg^{-1}\right) g + g^{-1}\, dg, \\ dg^{-1} &= -\, g^{-1}\, \left(dg\right)\, g^{-1}. \end{aligned} \]

Taking the exterior derivative of \(\theta\) :

\[ \begin{aligned} d\theta = d\!\left(g^{-1} \wedge dg\right) &= \left(dg^{-1}\right)\wedge dg \\ &= -\,g^{-1}\left(dg\right) g^{-1}\wedge dg \\ &= -\,\left(g^{-1} dg\right)\wedge\left(g^{-1} dg\right) \\ &= -\,\theta\wedge\theta. \end{aligned} \]

The grouping explicitly proves the identity. \(\blacksquare\)

3.2 Transformation of the Chern–Simons Action

Unlike the Maxwell term, the Chern-Simons action is not strictly gauge invariant. Let's compute the exact difference \(\mathcal{S}_{CS}[A^g] - \mathcal{S}_{CS}[A]\) assuming the manifold \(M\) is closed (meaning \(\partial M = 0\) ).

Step 1 — Curvature covariance. The nonabelian field strength transforms strictly covariantly:

\[ F^g = dA^g + A^g \wedge A^g = g^{-1} F\, g. \]

The inhomogeneous piece gracefully cancels out entirely by virtue of the Maurer-Cartan identity \(d\theta = -\theta\wedge\theta\) .

Step 2 — Chern–Simons descent identity. Expanding the action term by term yields:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{tr}\!\left(A^g\wedge dA^g\right) &= \mathrm{tr}\!\Big( \left(g^{-1}Ag + \theta\right)\wedge d\!\left(g^{-1}Ag + \theta\right)\Big), \\ \mathrm{tr}\!\left(A^g \wedge A^g \wedge A^g\right) &= \mathrm{tr}\!\Big( \left(g^{-1}Ag + \theta\right)^{\wedge 3}\Big). \end{aligned} \]

Using trace cyclicity, the covariance \(d(g^{-1}Ag) = g^{-1}(dA)g + g^{-1}[A,\theta]_\wedge\, g\) , and repeated applications of \(d\theta = -\theta\wedge\theta\) , all mixed \(A\) – \(\theta\) terms systematically organize into a total derivative, leaving a pure-gauge remainder:

\[ \mathrm{CS}(A^g) = \mathrm{CS}(A) - d\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left( A \wedge \theta \right) - \frac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left( \theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta \right). \]

Step 3 — Integration. Since we specified that \(M\) is a closed manifold, the total-derivative cross-term \(d\,\mathrm{tr}(A\wedge\theta)\) integrates exactly to zero by Stokes' theorem. Hence, the action transforms as:

\[ \mathcal{S}_{CS}[A^g] = \mathcal{S}_{CS}[A] - \frac{\kappa}{3}\int_M \mathrm{tr}\!\left(\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta\right). \]

4. The Winding Number Density

4.1 Definition and Topological Meaning

Definition 3.4.1 (Winding Number Density). The winding number density of the group map \(g: M \to G\) is:

\[ w(g) = \frac{1}{24\pi^2}\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\, \mathrm{tr}\!\left( g^{-1}\partial_\mu g\; g^{-1}\partial_\nu g\; g^{-1}\partial_\rho g \right). \]

In differential form language, this is compactly written as:

\[ w(g)\, d^3x = \frac{1}{24\pi^2}\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left(\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta\right). \]

For a simple compact Lie group, the third homotopy group is non-trivial: \(\pi_3(G) = \mathbb{Z}\) . Therefore, the total integral over the spacetime manifold strictly evaluates to an integer:

\[ N = \int_M w(g)\, d^3x. \]

This integer \(N\) is the degree of the map \(g\) , representing the topological winding number that definitively classifies the homotopy class \([g] \in \pi_3(G) \cong \mathbb{Z}\) .

4.2 Closedness of the Winding Form

For \(N\) to be a valid topological invariant, the 3-form \(\mathrm{tr}(\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta)\) must be closed.

\[ \begin{aligned} d\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left(\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta\right) &= 3\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left(d\theta \wedge \theta \wedge \theta\right) \\ &= -3\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left(\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta\right) = 0. \end{aligned} \]

The first equality relies on the Leibniz rule and trace cyclicity. The final vanishing, however, requires a precise antisymmetry argument. The form \(\theta\) is a Lie-algebra valued 1-form. If we cyclically permute the four 1-forms inside the trace of \(\theta^{\wedge 4}\) , moving the first form past the remaining three 1-forms, we pick up a sign of \((-1)^3 = -1\) . Therefore:

\[ \mathrm{tr}\!\left(\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta\right) = -\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left(\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta\right) = 0. \]

Thus, \(w(g)\) safely represents a nontrivial de Rham cohomology class whose periods are strict integers.

4.3 Relation to the Action Shift

Comparing the gauge transformation descent (Section 3.2) with the winding number definition:

\[ \mathcal{S}_{CS}[A^g] - \mathcal{S}_{CS}[A] = -\frac{\kappa}{3}\int_M \mathrm{tr}\!\left(\theta\wedge\theta\wedge\theta\right) = -\frac{\kappa}{3}\cdot 24\pi^2 \int_M w(g)\,d^3x. \]

Therefore, we arrive at the central classical result of nonabelian planar gauge theory: under a large gauge transformation (one with a non-zero winding number \(N \in \mathbb{Z}\) ), the Chern–Simons action shifts by a massive additive constant:

\[ \mathcal{S}_{CS} \;\to\; \mathcal{S}_{CS} - 8\pi^2\,\kappa\,N. \]

5. The Strict Quantization Condition

5.1 Physical Requirement

In quantum field theory, the classical action itself is not required to be gauge invariant. What must be strictly single-valued and invariant is the quantum amplitude entering the Feynman path integral:

\[ e^{\,i\mathcal{S}_{CS}}. \]

Under a large gauge transformation of winding number \(N\) :

\[ e^{\,i\mathcal{S}_{CS}[A^g]} = e^{\,i\left(\mathcal{S}_{CS}[A] - 8\pi^2\kappa N\right)} = e^{\,i\mathcal{S}_{CS}[A]}\; e^{-\,i\,8\pi^2\kappa N}. \]

5.2 The Quantization

For the path integral to be globally well-defined and gauge-independent, the anomaly phase must vanish:

\[ e^{-\,i\,8\pi^2\kappa N} = 1 \qquad \text{for all } N \in \mathbb{Z}. \]

Equivalently:

\[ 8\pi^2\,\kappa\,N \in 2\pi\,\mathbb{Z} \qquad \forall\, N \in \mathbb{Z}. \]

The strongest constraint comes from the \(N = 1\) sector:

\[ 8\pi^2\,\kappa = 2\pi\, n, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}. \]

Solving for \(\kappa\) yields the foundational level quantization:

\[ \kappa = \frac{2\pi\, n}{8\pi^2} = \frac{n}{4\pi}, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}. \]

5.3 Conclusion

Theorem 3.5.1 (Chern-Simons Level Quantization). In a nonabelian Chern-Simons theory defined on a closed manifold, global gauge invariance of the quantum path integral mandates that the coupling constant is strictly quantized:

\[ \kappa = \frac{n}{4\pi}, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}. \]

The integer \(n\) is known as the level of the Chern-Simons theory. Its rigorous discreteness guarantees that no continuous deformations of \(\kappa\) are permitted. The coupling constant lives strictly on the discrete topological lattice \(\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\mathbb{Z}\) , a direct consequence of the interplay between the generator normalization, the descent geometry of the Chern-Simons form, and the homotopy group \(\pi_3(G) = \mathbb{Z}\) .

3.2 Curvature

In the previous article, we've discussed about the notion of parallel transporting a vector from \(p\) to \(q\) along a curve \(C\) . However, the resulting vector in \(V_q\) , which is parallel transported by a vector in \(V_p\) depends on the choice of the curve \(C\) . Using this, we could define the intrinsic notion of curvature.

1. Riemann Curvature Tensor

First, let \(\nabla_a\) be a derivative operator. Let \(\omega_a\) be a dual vector field and let \(f\) be a smooth function. We calculate the action of two derivative operators applied to \(f\omega_a\) .

\[ \begin{aligned} \nabla_a \nabla_b \left ( f \omega_c \right ) &= \nabla_a \left ( \omega_c \nabla_b f + f \nabla_b \omega_c \right ) \\\\ &= \left ( \nabla_a \nabla_b f \right ) \omega_c + \nabla_b f \nabla_a \omega_c + \nabla_a f \nabla_b \omega_c + f \nabla_a \nabla_b \omega_c. \end{aligned} \]

We can also consider \(\nabla_b \nabla_a \left ( f \omega_c \right )\) . Then,

\[ \left ( \nabla_a \nabla_b - \nabla_b \nabla_a \right ) \left ( f \omega_c \right ) = f \left ( \nabla_a \nabla_b - \nabla_b \nabla_a \right ) \omega_c. \]

So, the tensor \(\left ( \nabla_a \nabla_b - \nabla_b \nabla_a \right ) \left ( f \omega_c \right )\) at point \(p\) depends only on the value of \(\omega_c\) at \(p\) . Consequently, \(\left ( \nabla_a \nabla_b - \nabla_b \nabla_a \right )\) defines a linear map from dual vectors at \(p\) to type \((0,3)\) tensors at \(p\) , i.e., its action is that of a tensor of type \((1,3)\) .

Definition 3.2.1 (Riemann Curvature Tensor). Thus, we have shown that there exists a tensor field \(R_{abc}{}^{d}\) such that for all dual vector fields \(\omega_c\) , we have

\[ \nabla_a \nabla_b \omega_c - \nabla_b \nabla_a \omega_c = R_{abc} {}^d \omega_d. \tag{*} \]

Here, \(R_{abc} {}^d\) is called the Riemann curvature tensor .

2. Riemann Curvature Tensor Measures Path Dependency

We first show that \(R_{abc}{}^d\) is related to the failure of a vector to return to its initial value when parallel transported around a small loop. We can conveniently construct a small closed loop at \(p \in \mathcal{M}\) by choosing a \(2\) -dimensional surface \(S\) through \(p\) and choosing coordinates \(t\) and \(s\) in the surface. Considering the loop formed by moving \(\Delta t\) along the \(s=0\) curve, followed by moving \(\Delta s\) along the \(t= \Delta t\) curve, and then moving back by \(\Delta t\) and \(\Delta s\) .

Let \(v^a\) be a vector at \(p\) (which is not necessarily tangent to \(S\) ) and let us parallel transport \(v^a\) around this closed loop. It is easy to compute the change in \(v^a\) by letting \(\omega_a\) be an arbitrary dual vector field and finding the change in the scalar \(v^a \omega_a\) . For small \(\Delta t\) , let there be a change of \(\delta_1\) in \(v^a \omega_a\) .

\[ \delta_1 = \left . \Delta t \frac{\partial}{\partial t} (v^a \omega_a ) \right \vert _{(\Delta t/2, 0)} \]

We may rewrite \(\delta_1\) as

\[ \delta_1 = \left . \Delta t T^b \nabla_b (v^a \omega_a) \right \vert_{(\Delta t/2 , 0)} = \left . \Delta t v^a T^b \nabla_b \omega_a \right \vert_{(\Delta t / 2 , 0)} \]

where \(T^b\) is the tangent to the curves of constant \(s\) and \(T^b \nabla_b v^a = 0\) . Similar expressions hold for \(\delta_2\) , \(\delta_3\) , \(\delta_4\) . Combining \(\delta_1\) and \(\delta_3\) , we get

\[ \delta_{1} + \delta_3 = \Delta t \left \{ \left . v^a T^b \nabla_b \omega_a \right \vert_{(\Delta t / 2 , 0)} - \left . v^a T^b \nabla_b \omega_a \right \vert_{(\Delta t / 2 , \Delta s)} \right \} \]

and \(\delta_2\) , \(\delta_4\) combines similarly. Since the terms in the brackets vanish as \(\Delta s \to 0\) , this shows that to first order in \(\Delta t\) and \(\Delta s\) , the total change in \(v^a \omega_a\) vanishes, i.e., parallel transport is path-independent to first order in \(\Delta t\) and \(\Delta s\) .

To evaluate the second order change in \(v^a \omega_a\) , we need to evaluate the term in brackets to first order. We do this by the following procedure.

  • Consider the curve \(t = \Delta t / 2\) and imagine parallel transporting \(v^a\) and \(T^b \nabla_b \omega_a\) along this curve from \((\Delta t / 2 , 0)\) to \((\Delta t/2 , \Delta s)\) .

  • Now, to first order in \(\Delta s\) , \(v^a\) at \((\Delta t / 2 , \Delta s)\) equals the parallel transport of \(v^a\) at \((\Delta t / 2 , 0)\) along this curve.

  • On the other hand, to first order, the term \(T^b \nabla_b \omega_a\) at \((\Delta t / 2 , \Delta s)\) will differ from the parallel transport of that quantity from \((\Delta t / 2 , 0)\) by amount \(\Delta s S^c \nabla_c (T^b \nabla_b \omega_a)\) , where \(S^c\) is the tangent to the curves of constant \(t\) .

Hence, the term in brackets is just this quantity contracted with \(v^a\) . Thus, to second order in \(\Delta t\) , \(\Delta s\) ,

\[ \delta_1 + \delta_3 = - \Delta t \Delta s v^a S^c \nabla_c (T^b \nabla_b \omega_a) \]

where, to this accuracy, we may evaluate all tensors at \(p\) . Adding the similar contribution from \(\delta_2\) and \(\delta_4\) , we find the total change as

\[ \begin{aligned} \delta (v^a \omega_a ) &= \Delta t \Delta s v^a \left \{T^c \nabla_c (S^b \nabla_b \omega_a) - S^c \nabla_c (T^b \nabla_b \omega_a ) \right \} \\\\ &= \Delta t \Delta s v^a T^c S^b (\nabla_c \nabla_b - \nabla_b \nabla_c) \omega_a \\\\ &= \Delta t \Delta s v^a T^c S^b R_{cba}{}^{d} \omega_d \end{aligned} \]

where we used that the coordinate vector fields \(T^a\) and \(S^b\) commute. This equation holds for all \(\omega_a\) if and only if the total change in \(v^a\) is

\[ \delta v^a = \Delta t \Delta s v^d T^c S^b R_{cbd}{}^a. \]

So, this shows that the Riemann tensor directly measures the path dependence of parallel transport.

3. Properties of Riemann Tensor

Using \((*)\) , we may express the action of the commutator of derivative operators on arbitrary tensor fields in terms of the Riemann tensor. Let \(t^a\) be a vector field and let \(\omega_a\) be a dual vector field. Then,

\[ \begin{aligned} 0 &= \left ( \nabla_a \nabla_b - \nabla_b \nabla_a \right ) \left ( t^c \omega_c \right ) \\\\ &= \nabla_a \left ( \omega_c \nabla_b t^c + t^c \nabla_b \omega_c \right ) - \nabla_b \left ( \omega_c \nabla_a t^c + t^c \nabla_a \omega_c \right ) \\\\ &= \omega_c \left ( \nabla_a \nabla_b - \nabla_b \nabla_a \right ) t^c + t^c \left ( \nabla_a \nabla_b - \nabla_b \nabla_a \right ) \omega_c \\\\ &= \omega_c \left ( \nabla_a \nabla_b - \nabla_b \nabla_a \right ) t^c + t^c \omega_d R_{abc}{}^d. \end{aligned} \]

Thus, we get

\[ \left ( \nabla_a \nabla_b - \nabla_b \nabla_a \right ) t^c = - R_{abd}{}^c t^d. \]

By induction, for an arbitrary tensor field \(T^{c_1 \cdots c_k}{}_{d_1 \cdots d_l}\) , we find

\[ \left ( \nabla_a \nabla_b - \nabla_b \nabla_a \right ) T^{c_1 \cdots c_k}_{d_1 \cdots d_l} = - \sum_{i=1}^{k} R_{abe}{}^{c_i} T^{c_1 \cdots e \cdots c_k}{}_{d_1 \cdots d_l} + \sum_{j=1}^{l} R_{abd_j} {}^{e} T^{c_1 \cdots c_k}{}_{d_1 \cdots e \cdots d_l}. \]

Now we look for key properties of the Riemann tensor.

  1. \(R_{abc}{}^d = - R_{bac}{}^d\)

  2. \(R_{[abc]}{}^{d} = 0\)

  3. For the derivative operator \(\nabla_a\) naturally associated with the metric, i.e., \(\nabla_a g_{bc} = 0\) , we get

\[ R_{abcd} = - R_{abdc}. \]
  1. The Bianchi identity holds:
\[ \nabla_{[a} R_{bc]d}{}^e = \frac{1}{6} \left( \nabla_a R_{bcd}{}^e + \nabla_b R_{cad}{}^e + \nabla_c R_{abd}{}^e - \nabla_a R_{cbd}{}^e - \nabla_b R_{acd}{}^e - \nabla_c R_{bad}{}^e \right) = 0. \]

4. Trace Part and Trace-free Part of Riemann Tensor

It is useful to decompose the Riemann tensor into a trace part and a trace-free part. By antisymmetry properties, the trace of the Riemann tensor over its first two or last two indices vanishes.

Definition 3.2.2 (Ricci Tensor and Scalar Curvature). However, its trace over the second and fourth (or equivalently, the first and third) indices defines the Ricci tensor \(R_{ac}\) ,

\[ R_{ac} = R_{abc}{}^{b}. \]

The scalar curvature \(R\)

\[ R = R_{a}{}^a, \]

is defined as the trace of the Ricci tensor .

Since we know that \(R_{abcd} = R_{cdab}\) , \(R_{ac}\) satisfies

\[ R_{ac} = R_{ca} \]

which is the symmetry property.

Definition 3.2.3 (Weyl Tensor). The trace-free part is called the Weyl tensor , \(C_{abcd}\) , and is defined by the equation

\[ R_{abcd} = C_{abcd} + \frac{2}{n-2} \left ( g_{a[c}R_{d]b} - g_{b[c}R_{d]a} \right ) - \frac{2}{(n-1)(n-2)} R g_{a[c} g_{d]b} \]

for manifolds of dimension \(n \ge 3\) .

The Weyl tensor satisfies the symmetry properties 1, 2, 3 of the Riemann tensor as well as being trace-free for all its indices. It also behaves in a very simple manner under conformal transformations of the metric and for this reason, it is sometimes called the conformal tensor .

5. Einstein Tensor

Contraction of the Bianchi identity leads to an important equation satisfied by \(R_{ab}\) . We find

\[ \nabla_a R_{bcd}{}^a + \nabla_b R_{cd} - \nabla_c R_{bd} = 0. \]

Raising the index \(d\) with the metric and contracting over \(b\) and \(d\) , we obtain

\[ \nabla_a R_{c}{}^a + \nabla_b R_c {}^b - \nabla_c R = 0 \]

or in a simpler form,

\[ \nabla^a G_{ab} = 0 \]

Definition 3.2.4 (Einstein Tensor). where

\[ G_{ab} = R_{ab} - \frac{1}{2} R g_{ab}. \]

The tensor \(G_{ab}\) is called the Einstein tensor .

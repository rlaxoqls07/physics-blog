3.1 Derivative Operators and Parallel Transport

1. Definitions

Definition 3.1.1 (Derivative Operator). A derivative operator \(\nabla\) , which is sometimes called a covariant derivative on a manifold \(\mathcal{M}\) , is a map which takes each smooth tensor field of type \((k,l)\) to a smooth tensor field of type \((k,l+1)\) and satisfies the five properties below:

  • Linearity: For all \(A, B \in \mathcal{T}(k, l)\) and \(\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R}\) ,
\[ \nabla_c(\alpha A^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l} + \beta B^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l}) = \alpha \nabla_c A^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l} + \beta \nabla_c B^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l} \]
  • Leibniz rule: For all \(A \in \mathcal{T}(k, l)\) , \(B \in \mathcal{T}(k', l')\) ,
\[ \begin{aligned} \nabla_e[A^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l} B^{c_1 \cdots c_{k'}}{}_{d_1 \cdots d_{l'}}] &= [\nabla_e A^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l}]B^{c_1 \cdots c_{k'}}{}_{d_1 \cdots d_{l'}} \\ &\quad + A^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l}[\nabla_e B^{c_1 \cdots c_{k'}}{}_{d_1 \cdots d_{l'}}] \quad . \end{aligned} \]
  • Commutativity with contraction: For all \(A \in \mathcal{T}(k, l)\) ,
\[ \nabla_d(A^{a_1 \cdots c \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots c \cdots b_l}) = \nabla_d A^{a_1 \cdots c \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots c \cdots b_l} \]
  • Consistency with the notion of tangent vectors as directional derivatives on scalar fields: For all \(f \in \mathcal{F}\) and all \(t^a \in V_p\) ,
\[ t(f) = t^a \nabla_a f \quad . \]
  • Torsion free: 1 For all \(f \in \mathcal{F}\) ,
\[ \nabla_a \nabla_b f = \nabla_b \nabla_a f \quad . \]

\(\;\)

If we have \(T^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l} \in \mathcal{T}(k,l)\) , we denote the tensor field resulting from the action of \(\nabla\) on \(T\) by \(\nabla_c \, T^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l}\) .

Conditions 4 and 5 with the Leibniz rule allow us to derive a simple expression for the commutator of two vector fields \(v^a\) , \(w^b\) in terms of any derivative operator \(\nabla_a\) . Applied to any smooth function \(f\) ,

\[ \begin{aligned} [v, w](f) &= v\{w(f)\} - w\{v(f)\} \\\\ &= v^a \nabla_a (w^b \nabla_b f) - w^a \nabla_a (v^b \nabla_b f) \\\\ &= \{v^a \nabla_a w^b - w^a \nabla_a v^b\} \nabla_b f . \end{aligned} \]

Thus we have

\[ \left [ v , w \right ]^b = v^a \nabla_{a} w^b - w^a \nabla_{a} v^b. \]

2. Existence and Uniqueness of Derivative Operator

Now we show that derivative operators exist. Let \(\psi\) be a coordinate system and let \(\{\partial / \partial x^\mu \}\) and \(\{dx^\mu\}\) be associated coordinate bases. Then in the region covered by these coordinates, we may define a derivative operator, \(\partial_a\) , called an ordinary derivative as follows.

For any smooth tensor field \(T^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l}\) we take its components \(T^{\mu_1 \cdots \mu_k}{}_{\nu_1 \cdots \nu_l}\) in this coordinate basis and define \(\partial_c T^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l}\) to be the tensor whose components in this coordinate basis are the partial derivatives \(\partial (T^{\mu_1 \cdots \mu_k}{}_{\nu_1 \cdots \nu_l}) / \partial x^\sigma\) . Then, the five conditions follow from the properties of partial derivatives. Also, by equality of mixed partial derivatives, condition 5 holds for all tensor fields, not just scalar fields. Thus, given a system \(\psi\) , we can construct an associated derivative operator \(\partial_a\) .

What happens if we choose a different coordinate system \(\psi'\) ? This will yield a different derivative operator \(\partial_a '\) , that is, the components of the tensor \(\partial_c T^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l}\) in the new coordinates will not be equal to the partial derivatives of the primed components of \(T^{a_1 \cdots a_k}{}_{b_1 \cdots b_l}\) with respect to the primed coordinates. Thus, a given ordinary derivative operator is coordinate dependent, i.e., it is not associated with the intrinsic structure of the manifold.

Now we consider the uniqueness of derivative operators. By condition 4, any two derivative operators \(\nabla_a\) and \(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\) must agree in their action on scalar fields. We consider the difference between derivative operators in the next section.

3. Difference between Derivative Operators

To investigate their possible disagreements, let \(\omega_b\) be a dual vector field and consider the difference \(\tilde{\nabla}_a (f\omega_b) - \nabla_a (f\omega_b)\) for an arbitrary scalar field \(f\) . Then,

\[ \begin{aligned} \tilde{\nabla}_{a} (f \omega_{b}) - \nabla_{a} (f \omega_{b}) &= \left ( \tilde{\nabla}_a f \right ) \omega_b + f \tilde{\nabla}_a \omega_b - \left ( \nabla_a f \right ) \omega_b - f \nabla_a \omega_b \\ &= f \left ( \tilde{\nabla}_a \omega_b - \nabla_a \omega_b \right ) \end{aligned} \]

At a point \(p\) , \(\tilde{\nabla}_a \omega_b\) and \(\nabla_a \omega_b\) each depend on how \(\omega_b\) changes as one moves away from \(p\) . However, the equation above shows that their difference depends only on the value of \(\omega_b\) at the point \(p\) . To see this, suppose that \(\omega_b '\) equals \(\omega_b\) at \(p\) and show that we get the same answer if we replace \(\omega_{b}\) by \(\omega_b '\) . Since \(\omega_b ' - \omega_b\) vanishes at \(p\) , we can find smooth functions \(f_{(\alpha)}\) which vanish at \(p\) and smooth dual vector fields, \(\mu_b ^{(\alpha)}\) such that

\[ \omega_{b}' - \omega_{b} = \sum_{\alpha=1}^n f_{(\alpha)} \mu_{b} ^{(\alpha)}. \]

Hence,

\[ \begin{aligned} \tilde{\nabla}_a \left ( \omega_b ' - \omega_b \right ) - \nabla_a \left ( \omega_b ' - \omega_b \right ) &= \sum_\alpha \left \{ \tilde{\nabla}_a \left ( f_{(\alpha)} \mu_b ^{(\alpha)} \right ) - \nabla_a \left ( f_{(\alpha)} \mu_b ^{(\alpha)} \right ) \right \} \\ &= \sum_\alpha f_{(\alpha)} \left \{\tilde{\nabla}_a \mu_b ^{(\alpha)} - \nabla_a \mu_b ^{(\alpha)} \right \} = 0 \end{aligned} \]

since each \(f_{(\alpha)} = 0\) at \(p\) . Thus, we have

\[ \tilde{\nabla}_a \omega_b ' - \nabla_a \omega_b ' = \tilde{\nabla}_{a} \omega_b - \nabla_a \omega_b. \]

Thus, we have shown that \(\tilde{\nabla}_a - \nabla_a\) defines a map of dual vectors at \(p\) to tensors of type \((0,2)\) at \(p\) . Then, \(\left ( \tilde{\nabla}_a - \nabla_a \right )\) defines a tensor of type \((1,2)\) at \(p\) , which we denote as \(C^c {}_{ab}\) . To summarize, we have shown that given any two derivative operators \(\tilde{\nabla}_a\) and \(\nabla_a\) there exists a tensor field \(C^c {}_{ab}\) such that

\[ \nabla_{a} \omega_{b} = \tilde{\nabla}_{a} \omega_{b} - C^c {}_{ab} \, \omega_{c}. \]

We have a symmetry property of \(C^c {}_{ab}\) . Take \(\omega_b = \nabla_b f = \tilde{\nabla}_b f\) . Then,

\[ \nabla_a \nabla_b f = \tilde{\nabla}_a \tilde{\nabla}_b f - C^c {}_{ab} \nabla_c f. \]

Since both \(\nabla_a \nabla_b f\) and \(\tilde{\nabla}_a \tilde{\nabla}_b f\) are symmetric in \(a\) and \(b\) , \(C^c {}_{ab}\) must also have the property

\[ C^c {}_{ab} = C^c {}_{ba}. \]

For a vector field \(t^a\) and \(1\) -form field \(\omega_a\) , property 4 gives that

\[ \left ( \tilde{\nabla}_a - \nabla_a \right ) \left ( \omega_b t^b \right ) = 0. \]

On the other hand, by the Leibniz rule, we have

\[ \left ( \tilde{\nabla}_a - \nabla_a \right ) \left ( \omega_b t^b \right ) = \left ( C^c {}_{ab} \omega_c \right ) t^b + \omega_b \left ( \tilde{\nabla}_a - \nabla_a \right ) t^b. \]

Substituting some indices, we get

\[ \omega_b \left [ \left ( \tilde{\nabla}_a - \nabla_a \right ) t^b + C^b {}_{ac} t^c \right ] = 0 \]

for all \(\omega_b\) . This implies

\[ \nabla_a t^b = \tilde{\nabla}_a t^b + C^b {}_{ac} t^c. \]

In a similar manner, we can derive the general formula for the action of \(\nabla_a\) on an arbitrary tensor field in terms of \(\tilde{\nabla}_a\) and \(C^c {}_{ab}\) . For \(T \in \mathcal{T}(k,l)\) , we find

\[ \nabla_a T^{b_1 \cdots b_k} {}_{c_1 \cdots c_l} = \tilde{\nabla}_a T^{b_1 \cdots b_k} {}_{c_1 \cdots c_l} + \sum_i C^{b_i} {}_{ad} T^{b_1 \cdots d \cdots b_k}{}_{c_1 \cdots c_l} - \sum_j C^d {}_{ac_j} T^{b_1 \cdots b_l}{}_{c_1 \cdots d \cdots c_l}. \tag{*} \]

Thus, the difference between the two derivative operators \(\nabla_a\) and \(\tilde{\nabla}_a\) is characterized by the tensor field \(C^c {}_{ab}\) .

Conversely, it is easy to check that if \(\tilde{\nabla}_a\) is a derivative operator and \(C^c {}_{ab}\) is an arbitrary smooth tensor field which is symmetric in its lower indices, then \(\nabla_a\) defined by \((*)\) will also be a derivative operator. So, on an \(n\) -dimensional manifold, \(C^c {}_{ab}\) has \(n^2 (n+1)/2\) independent components to be specified at each point.

4. Christoffel Symbol

Definition 3.1.2 (Christoffel Symbol). The important application of \((*)\) is when \(\tilde{\nabla}_a\) is an ordinary derivative operator \(\partial_a\) . In this case, the tensor field \(C^c {}_{ab}\) is denoted as \(\Gamma^c {}_{ab}\) and called a Christoffel symbol .

For example, we write

\[ \nabla_a t^b = \partial_a t^b + \Gamma^b {}_{ac} t^c. \]

Since we already know how to take \(\partial_a\) , we only need to know about \(\Gamma^b {}_{ac}\) to know how to compute the derivative \(\nabla_a\) . Note that we have used a coordinate system to define \(\partial_a\) . So, if we change coordinates, we also change our ordinary derivative operator \(\partial_a\) to \(\partial_a '\) , and thus we have our tensor \(\Gamma^c {}_{ab}\) transformed to a new tensor \({\Gamma '}^c {}_{ab}\) . Hence, the components of \(\Gamma^c {}_{ab}\) will not be related to the components of \({\Gamma'}^c {}_{ab}\) in the primed coordinates by the tensor transformation law.

We learn how to calculate the Christoffel symbols in the next few sections.

5. Parallel Transport

Definition 3.1.3 (Parallel Transport & Connection). Given a derivative operator \(\nabla_a\) , we can define the notion of parallel transport of a vector along a curve \(C\) with a tangent \(t^a\) . A vector \(v^a\) given at each point on the curve is said to be parallelly transported as one moves along the curve if the equation

\[ t^a \nabla_{a} v^b = 0 \]

is satisfied along the curve.

More generally, one can define the parallel transport of a tensor by

\[ t^a \nabla_a T^{b_1 \cdots b_k}{}_{c_1 \cdots c_l} = 0. \]

Choosing a coordinate system, we can express this as the following:

\[ t^a \partial_a v^b + t^a \Gamma^b {}_{ac} v^c = 0 \]

Or, equivalently, in terms of components in the coordinate basis and the parameter \(t\) along the curve,

\[ \frac{dv^\nu}{dt} + \sum_{\mu , \nu} t^\mu \Gamma^\nu {}_{\mu \lambda} v^\lambda = 0. \]

So, the parallel transport of \(v^a\) depends only on the values of \(v^a\) on the curve, so we may consider the parallel transport properties of vectors defined only along the curve as opposed to vector fields. Also, by the uniqueness of solution of ODE with a given initial value of \(v^a\) , a vector at a point \(p\) on the curve uniquely defines a parallel transported vector everywhere else on the curve.

Now, we may use this notion of parallel transport to identify the tangent spaces \(V_p\) and \(V_q\) of points \(p\) and \(q\) if we are given a derivative operator and a curve connecting \(p\) and \(q\) . The mathematical structure arising from such a curve dependent identification of the tangent spaces of different points is called a connection . Conversely, one could start with the general notion of a connection and use it to define the notion of derivative operator.

6. Parallel Transport with Metric

Given only the manifold structure, many distinct derivative operators are possible and no one of them is naturally preferred over the others. We should now give a metric \(g_{ab}\) on the manifold. Then, there is a natural choice of derivative operator, which is uniquely picked out. This is because the metric gives rise to a natural condition which we may impose on the notion of parallel transport.

Given two vectors \(v^a\) and \(w^a\) , we would like to require that their inner product \(g_{ab} v^a w^b\) remain unchanged if we parallel transport them along any curve. Thus, we require

\[ t^a \nabla_{a} \left (g_{bc} v^b w^c \right ) = 0 \]

for \(v^b\) and \(w^c\) which satisfy \(t^a \nabla_a v^b = 0\) which was introduced earlier. Using the Leibniz rule,

\[ t^a v^b w^c \nabla_a g_{bc} = 0. \]

This equation holds for all curves and parallelly transported vectors if and only if

\[ \nabla_{a} g_{bc} = 0 \]

which is the additional condition we want to impose on \(\nabla_a\) . Then, this equation uniquely determines \(\nabla_a\) by the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1.1. Let \(g_{ab}\) be a metric. Then there exists a unique derivative operator \(\nabla_a\) satisfying \(\nabla_a g_{bc} = 0\) .

Proof. Let \(\tilde{\nabla}_a\) be any derivative operator. We would like to solve for \(C^c {}_{ab}\) so that the derivative operator determined by \(\tilde{\nabla}_a\) and \(C^c {}_{ab}\) will satisfy the required property. We will prove the theorem by showing that a unique solution for \(C^c {}_{ab}\) exists. By \((*)\) , \(C^c {}_{ab}\) must satisfy

\[ 0 = \nabla_a g_{bc} = \tilde{\nabla}_a g_{bc} - C^d {}_{ab} g_{dc} - C^d {}_{ac} g_{bd} \]

that is,

\[ C_{cab} + C_{bac} = \tilde{\nabla}_a g_{bc}. \]

Then, by substituting the indices, we also have

\[ \begin{aligned} C_{cba} + C_{abc} &= \tilde{\nabla}_b g_{ac} \\ C_{bca} + C_{acb} &= \tilde{\nabla}_c g_{ab}. \end{aligned} \]

By the three equations above,

\[ 2C_{cab} = \tilde{\nabla}_a g_{bc} + \tilde{\nabla}_b g_{ac} - \tilde{\nabla}_c g_{ab} \]

which is equivalent to

\[ C^c {}_{ab} = \frac{1}{2} g^{cd} \left \{\tilde{\nabla}_a g_{bd} + \tilde{\nabla}_b g_{ad} - \tilde{\nabla}_d g_{ab} \right \}. \tag{**} \]

This choice of \(C^c {}_{ab}\) solves \(\nabla_a g_{bc} = 0\) and is unique, which completes the proof. \(\blacksquare\)

Thus, a metric \(g_{ab}\) naturally determines a derivative operator \(\nabla_a\) . For the remainder of these articles, when a metric is present, we will always choose the derivative operator to be this natural one. Furthermore, \((*)\) and \((**)\) will tell us how to compute \(\nabla_a\) in terms of any other derivative operator \(\tilde{\nabla}_a\) . In particular, in terms of an ordinary derivative operator, the Christoffel symbol is

\[ \Gamma^c {}_{ab} = \frac{1}{2} g^{cd} \left \{\partial_a g_{bd} + \partial_b g_{ad} - \partial_d g_{ab} \right \} \]

and thus the coordinate basis components of the Christoffel symbol are

\[ \Gamma^\rho {}_{\mu \nu} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_\sigma g^{\rho \sigma} \left \{\frac{\partial g_{\nu \sigma}}{\partial x^\mu} + \frac{\partial g_{\mu \sigma}}{\partial x^\nu} - \frac{\partial g_{\mu \nu}}{\partial x^\sigma} \right \}. \]

Therefore, we can compute \(\Gamma^c {}_{ab}\) by taking partial derivatives of the coordinate basis components of the metric, and hence we can also compute \(\nabla_a\) .

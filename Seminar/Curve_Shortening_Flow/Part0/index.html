0. Introduction

0.1 Introduction to the Lecture

This is a lecture note written from the 2026 International Summer School for Advanced Undergraduate Students , which is held at KAIST, Daejeon.

This lecture, Curve Shortening Flow , was given by Professor Beomjun Choi, KAIST. His research interest lies in the intersection of differential geometry and partial differential equations. The subject of curve shortening flow is one of the premier topics residing precisely in this intersection.

We will omit some foundational knowledge of PDEs during this lecture, as explaining the full rigorous theory of partial differential equations would take several semesters.

Before we introduce our main topic, the curve shortening flow, we will briefly dive into the basic notion of the heat equation.

0.2 Heat Equation

Consider a function \(u(\theta, t)\) , where \(\theta \in S^1 = \mathbb{R} / 2\pi \mathbb{Z}\) . The heat equation is given by

\[ \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial ^2 u}{\partial \theta \partial \theta} \]

or equivalently,

\[ u_t = u_{\theta \theta}. \]

To solve this PDE, we require an initial condition:

\[ u(\theta, 0) = u_0 (\theta). \]

We solve this heat equation with the initial value in \(S^1\) by writing

\[ u_0 (\theta) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left ( a_n \sin (n \theta) + b_n \cos (n \theta) \right ) + b_0 \]

which we can always express in this Fourier expansion form provided \(u_0\) is an \(L^2\) function. Then, we can easily check that

\[ u(\theta, t) = \sum_{n\ge 1} e^{-n^2 t} \left ( a_n \sin (n \theta) + b_n \cos (n \theta) \right ) + b_0 \]

is a valid solution for the heat equation. Notice that all the terms except the constant \(b_0\) decay exponentially as time evolves. Intuitively, adopting the heat equation roughly corresponds to the geometric process of smoothing our curve.

3. More on Singularity Analysis

3.1 Gaussian Density and Local Regularity

Definition 3.1.1 (Gaussian density). The Gaussian density at \(X_0 = (x_0 , t_0)\) with scale \(\gamma > 0\) is defined by

\[ \Theta(\Gamma_t , X_0 , \gamma) = \int_{\Gamma_{t_0 - \gamma^2}}\rho_{X_0} (x , t_0 - \gamma^2 ) dx \; \left ( = \Phi_{X_0} (t_0 - \gamma^2) \right ) \]

With this localized mass measure in hand, we can establish a crucial regularity condition that restricts the maximum curvature whenever the density is sufficiently close to that of a flat line.

Theorem 3.1.2 (Local regularity theorem) 1 . There exist \(\varepsilon > 0\) , \(C< \infty\) such that the following holds. Let \(\Gamma_t\) be the curve shortening flow for \(t \in (t_0 - 4 \gamma^2 , t_0)\) . If \(\sup \Theta(\Gamma , Y , \gamma) \le 1+ \varepsilon\) , for \(Y \in B_r (x_0) \times (t_0 - \gamma^2 ,t_0) := P_\gamma (x_0 , t_0)\) , then

\[ \sup_{P_{\gamma/2}(x_0 , t_0)} \kappa \le \frac{C}{\gamma}. \]

Proof. Assume for the sake of contradiction that the theorem is false. Then, there exists a sequence of curve shortening flows, \(\Gamma_t^i\) for \(t \in (-4, 0 )\) , such that:

  • \(\Theta ( \Gamma_t^i , X , 1) < 1 + \frac{1}{i}\) for all \(X \in P_1 (0)\) ,
  • and there exists \(X_i \in P_{1/2} (0)\) such that \(\kappa (X_i) \ge i\) .

Claim: There exists a spacetime point \(Y_i = (y_i, t_i) \in P_{3/4} (0)\) with \(\kappa (Y_i) := \kappa_i \ge i\) such that \(\kappa \le 2 \kappa_i\) on the parabolic neighborhood \(P_{\frac{i}{10 \kappa_i}} (Y_i)\) .

(The proof of this claim follows via the point selection argument, deferred below).

Assuming the claim holds, we define our parabolically rescaled flow centered at \(Y_i\) as:

\[ \tilde{\Gamma}_t^i = \kappa_i \left( \Gamma_{t_i + \frac{t}{\kappa_i^2}}^i - y_i \right). \]

Under this parabolic rescaling, the geometry and bounds transform such that:

\[ \tilde{\kappa}^i (0,0) = 1 , \quad \text{and} \quad \tilde{\kappa}^i \le 2 \quad \text{on} \quad P_{\frac{i}{10}} (0). \]

Because the rescaled curvature is uniformly bounded by 2 on expanding domains (since \(i \to \infty\) ), all higher-order derivatives are also uniformly bounded. Thus, we can extract a smoothly convergent limit sequence, denoted as \(\tilde{\Gamma}_t^\infty\) , which is an eternal solution.

Moreover, by the density assumption of the theorem, taking the limit \(i \to \infty\) forces the Gaussian density of the limit flow to be exactly 1 at all scales:

\[ \Theta(\tilde{\Gamma}_t^\infty , X , r) = 1 \quad \forall X \in \mathbb{R}^2 \times (-\infty, 0], \,\, \forall r > 0. \]

By Huisken's monotonicity formula, the constancy of the density implies that \(\tilde{\Gamma}^\infty\) must be a self-shrinker. The only embedded self-shrinkers are the straight line and the shrinking circle.

However, the Gaussian density of a shrinking circle is strictly greater than 1, contradicting \(\Theta \equiv 1\) . Therefore, \(\tilde{\Gamma}^\infty\) must be a straight line. But a straight line has curvature identically zero, which directly contradicts our condition that \(\tilde{\kappa}^\infty(0,0) = 1\) . This completes the contradiction. \(\blacksquare\)

Consequently, this forces the realization that any sequence approaching a singularity under these density constraints cannot logically model anything other than a trivial flat line.

Remark. Alternatively, suppose that a rescaled solution \(\Lambda \Gamma_{t / \Lambda^2}\) converges to a straight line. By the multiplicity one conjecture, if \(\Lambda \gg 1\) , then \(\Theta (\Lambda \Gamma_{t/ \Lambda^2} , 0 , 1) \approx 1\) . Consequently, \(\Lambda \Gamma_{t/\Lambda^2}\) must have \(\kappa \le C\) , which yields a similar contradiction if we assume unbounded curvature. Therefore, a straight line cannot manifest as the singularity model in this context.

To rigorously justify the local curvature bounds that allowed us to extract the rescaled limit sequence above, we now detail the deferred point selection argument.

Proof of claim. This is an application of the point selection argument 2 .

We construct a sequence of points iteratively. Let \(Y_i^0 = X_i\) . By our initial assumptions, \(Y_i^0 \in P_{1/2}(0)\) and \(\kappa(Y_i^0) \ge i\) .

If \(Y_i^0\) satisfies the claim (i.e., \(\kappa \le 2\kappa(Y_i^0)\) on \(P_{\frac{i}{10\kappa(Y_i^0)}}(Y_i^0)\) ), we set \(Y_i = Y_i^0\) and terminate the sequence.

If not, there exists a point \(Y_i^1 \in P_{\frac{i}{10\kappa(Y_i^0)}}(Y_i^0)\) such that \(\kappa(Y_i^1) > 2\kappa(Y_i^0)\) .

Iterating this process, if \(Y_i^k\) does not satisfy the claim, we can find \(Y_i^{k+1} \in P_{\frac{i}{10\kappa(Y_i^k)}}(Y_i^k)\) such that \(\kappa(Y_i^{k+1}) > 2\kappa(Y_i^k)\) .

Observe that \(\kappa(Y_i^k) > 2^k \kappa(Y_i^0) \ge 2^k i\) . Since the curve shortening flow is smooth on the given time interval, the curvature must be finite on compact subsets, meaning this sequence cannot continue indefinitely. It must terminate at some finite step \(m\) . The terminal point \(Y_i^m\) satisfies the local curvature bound, so we define our desired point as \(Y_i = Y_i^m\) .

It remains to show that this terminal point \(Y_i\) actually remains within \(P_{3/4}(0)\) . By the triangle inequality for the parabolic distance, the distance from the initial point \(Y_i^0\) to \(Y_i^k\) is bounded by the sum of the radii of the nested neighborhoods:

\[ |Y_i^k - Y_i^0| \le \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \frac{i}{10\kappa(Y_i^j)}. \]

Since \(\kappa(Y_i^j) > 2^j \kappa(Y_i^0)\) , we can bound the series by a geometric progression:

\[ |Y_i^k - Y_i^0| \le \frac{i}{10\kappa(Y_i^0)} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} + \cdots \right) = \frac{i}{10\kappa(Y_i^0)} (2) = \frac{i}{5\kappa(Y_i^0)}. \]

Because the initial curvature \(\kappa(Y_i^0) \ge i\) , we have \(\frac{i}{\kappa(Y_i^0)} \le 1\) . Thus:

\[ |Y_i^k - Y_i^0| \le \frac{1}{5}. \]

Furthermore, since \(Y_i^0 = X_i \in P_{1/2}(0)\) , its distance to the origin is \(|Y_i^0 - 0| \le \frac{1}{2}\) . Therefore:

\[ |Y_i^k| \le |Y_i^0 - 0| + |Y_i^k - Y_i^0| \le \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{5} = \frac{7}{10} < \frac{3}{4}. \]

This confirms that \(Y_i \in P_{3/4}(0)\) , completing the proof of the claim. \(\blacksquare\)

Let \(\Gamma_t\) be a curve shortening flow. Suppose that the rescaled solution \(\Lambda \Gamma_{t / \Lambda^2}\) converges to a straight line. By multiplicity one conjecture, if \(\Lambda \gg 1\) , \(\Theta (\Lambda \Gamma_{t/ \Lambda^2} , 0 , 1) \approx 1\) . Then, \(\Lambda \Gamma_{t/\Lambda^2}\) has \(\kappa \le C\) , which is a contradiction. Therefore, a straight line cannot be our solution.

3.2 Huisken's Distance Comparison Principle

Theorem 3.2.1 (Huisken distance comparison) 3 . For a curve shortening flow \(\Gamma_t\) , \(t \ge 0\) , define

\[ l(x,y,t) = l_t (\gamma(x,t) , \gamma (y,t) ), \quad d(x,y,t) = |\gamma(x,t) - \gamma(y,t)| \]

where \(l_t\) is the intrinsic distance. Also, define

\[ R(t) = \sup_{\begin{smallmatrix} x \ne y\\ x,y\in S^1 \end{smallmatrix}} \frac{L(t)}{\pi d(x,y,t)} \cdot \sin \left (\frac{\pi l(x,y,t)}{L(t)} \right ). \]

Then, \(R(t)\) is monotone decreasing.

Because this functional \(R(t)\) is bounded entirely by its initial geometry at \(t=0\) , it immediately grants us a uniform upper bound on the ratio of intrinsic to extrinsic distance across the evolving curve.

Remark. Then, we can bound \(l/d\) :

\[ \frac{l}{d} = \underbrace{\frac{L}{\pi d} \sin \left ( \frac{\pi l}{L} \right )}_{\le R(0)} \cdot \frac{\frac{l \pi}{L}}{\sin \left ( \frac{\pi l}{L} \right )} \le C \cdot R(0). \]

Note that \(\frac{L}{\pi d} \sin \left ( \frac{\pi l}{L} \right )\) is bounded by Theorem 3.2.1.

3.3 Type I and Type II Singularities

Lemma 3.3.1.

\[ \frac{d}{dt} \int_{\Gamma_t} |\kappa| \, ds = -2 \sum_{\{x \mid \kappa(x,t) = 0\}} |\kappa_s(x,t)| \]

where the set of zeroes is finite, or \(\kappa \equiv 0\) . Consequently, the limit

\[ \lim_{t \nearrow T (=t_0)} \int_{\Gamma_t} |\kappa| \, ds \]

exists.

The fact that this total absolute curvature integral remains definitively bounded as we approach the singular time provides the key analytical constraint needed to rule out rapidly forming singularities.

Proof of Theorem that singularities are Type I. Suppose not. That is, assume

\[ \sup_{t < T} \sqrt{T-t} \max_{\Gamma_t} |\kappa| = \infty. \]

For all \(k \in \mathbb{N}\) , choose \((y_k, t_k)\) such that it achieves the maximum for

\[ \max_{t \in \left[0, T - \frac{1}{k}\right]} \max_{\Gamma_t} \left( T - \frac{1}{k} - t \right) \cdot \kappa^2. \]

Denote \(\kappa(y_k, t_k) := \lambda_k\) . (This is an application of Hamilton's point-picking method 4 ).

We then define the parabolically rescaled flow:

\[ \tilde{\Gamma}_t^i = \lambda_i \left( \Gamma_{t_i + \frac{t}{\lambda_i^2}} - y_i \right). \]

Then, because of the bounds established by the point-picking method, we can extract a smoothly converging limit sequence as \(i \to \infty\) :

\[ \tilde{\Gamma}_t^i \longrightarrow \Gamma_t^\infty \quad \text{for} \quad -\infty < t < +\infty. \]

This limit flow \(\Gamma_t^\infty\) is an eternal solution, and it inherently satisfies the following curvature properties:

\[ |\kappa^\infty| \le 1 \quad \text{everywhere, and} \quad |\kappa^\infty(0,0)| = 1. \]

Claim: The limit flow \(\Gamma_t^\infty\) is convex, meaning \(\kappa^\infty > 0\) .

Assuming the claim that \(\Gamma_t^\infty\) is strictly convex ( \(\kappa^\infty > 0\) ), we can now completely classify this limit flow. By our parabolic rescaling construction, \(\Gamma_t^\infty\) is an eternal solution (meaning it exists for \(t \in (-\infty, \infty)\) ) that attains its global maximum curvature \(\kappa^\infty(0,0) = 1\) .

The only completely convex, eternal solutions to the curve shortening flow in the plane are the shrinking circle and the translating Grim Reaper. However, a shrinking circle collapses to a point in finite time, meaning it cannot exist for all \(t \in \mathbb{R}\) . Therefore, the limit \(\Gamma_t^\infty\) must be the Grim Reaper.

A Grim Reaper with a maximum curvature of \(1\) translates along an axis and is asymptotically bounded by two parallel lines separated by an extrinsic distance of \(d = \pi\) .

Suppose we pick two points on the opposite asymptotic branches of the Grim Reaper. Their extrinsic spatial distance in \(\mathbb{R}^2\) , denoted as \(d\) , is strictly bounded by \(\pi\) . However, the intrinsic distance along the curve between these two points, denoted as \(l\) , approaches \(\infty\) . Thus, on the limit curve \(\Gamma_t^\infty\) , the chord-arc ratio is unbounded:

\[ \frac{l}{d} \longrightarrow \infty. \]

This brings us to the fundamental contradiction. For the original simple closed curve \(\Gamma_t\) , Theorem 3.2.1 guarantees that the chord-arc ratio (intrinsic distance over extrinsic distance) is uniformly bounded by some constant \(C\) for all \(t < T\) :

\[ \frac{l}{d} < C. \]

Because this geometric ratio is perfectly invariant under parabolic rescaling, the rescaled sequence \(\tilde{\Gamma}_t^i\) must also obey \(\frac{l}{d} < C\) . Since \(\tilde{\Gamma}_t^i\) converges smoothly (with multiplicity 1) to the Grim Reaper, the limit Grim Reaper must also satisfy this bound, which we have shown is impossible.

Therefore, our initial assumption that the singularity is Type II is false. We conclude that all singularities for embedded curves under curve shortening flow must be Type I. \(\blacksquare\)

To finalize the classification of this limit geometry, we must verify the claim that the eternal limit flow is indeed completely convex.

Proof of claim. We want to show that the eternal limit flow \(\Gamma_t^\infty\) is strictly convex ( \(\kappa^\infty > 0\) ).

Fix an arbitrary time interval \([a, b]\) with \(-\infty < a < b < \infty\) . Using the lemma we established earlier for the evolution of the total absolute curvature, we integrate this quantity over the given time interval for our rescaled sequence \(\tilde{\Gamma}_t^i\) . Note that the time \(t\) in the rescaled flow corresponds to \(t_i + \frac{t}{\lambda_i^2}\) in the original flow. By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:

\[ -2 \int_a^b \sum_{\{x \mid \tilde{\kappa}_i(x,t) = 0\}} |(\tilde{\kappa}_i)_s| \, dt = \left[ \int_{\Gamma_t} |\kappa| \, ds \right]_{t = t_i + \frac{b}{\lambda_i^2}} - \left[ \int_{\Gamma_t} |\kappa| \, ds \right]_{t = t_i + \frac{a}{\lambda_i^2}}. \]

As established in our lemma, \(\int_{\Gamma_t} |\kappa| \, ds\) is monotonically decreasing and bounded strictly below, meaning its limit as \(t \nearrow T\) absolutely exists. As \(i \to \infty\) , we know that \(t_i \to T\) and \(\lambda_i \to \infty\) . Consequently, both time evaluations on the right-hand side converge to the singular time \(T\) . Therefore, the difference between these integrals must vanish:

\[ \lim_{i \to \infty} \left( \left[ \int_{\Gamma_t} |\kappa| \, ds \right]_{t = t_i + \frac{b}{\lambda_i^2}} - \left[ \int_{\Gamma_t} |\kappa| \, ds \right]_{t = t_i + \frac{a}{\lambda_i^2}} \right) = 0. \]

Taking \(i \to \infty\) for the left-hand side yields the behavior of the limit flow \(\Gamma_t^\infty\) . Extending our arbitrary interval \([a, b]\) to the entire real line \((-\infty, \infty)\) , we obtain:

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty \sum_{\{x \mid \kappa^\infty(x,t) = 0\}} |\kappa^\infty_s| \, dt = 0. \]

Because the integrand is strictly non-negative, this integral evaluating to zero implies a strong geometric constraint: at any spacetime point where the limit curvature vanishes ( \(\kappa^\infty = 0\) ), its spatial derivative must also vanish ( \(\kappa^\infty_s = 0\) ).

Graphically, if \(\kappa^\infty\) were to change sign (crossing the \(s\) -axis), it would exhibit a non-zero derivative at the root, which we have just proven is impossible. Thus, if the curvature graph ever touches \(0\) , it must be perfectly tangent to the axis. By the strong maximum principle for the curvature evolution equation \(\kappa_t = \kappa_{ss} + \kappa^3\) , if \(\kappa^\infty = 0\) and \(\kappa^\infty_s = 0\) at any point, then the curvature must be identically zero ( \(\kappa^\infty \equiv 0\) ) everywhere.

However, our blow-up sequence construction guarantees that the maximum curvature is preserved at the origin, specifically \(|\kappa^\infty(0,0)| = 1\) . This means \(\kappa^\infty\) cannot be identically zero. Since it cannot be identically zero and it cannot cross the axis, it must be strictly positive or strictly negative everywhere. By our standard inward-pointing orientation convention for closed curves, we conclude that \(\kappa^\infty > 0\) .

Hence, the limit flow \(\Gamma_t^\infty\) is strictly convex. \(\blacksquare\)
